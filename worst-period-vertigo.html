The Worst Period: My Experience With Vertigo and Severe Neck Problems in 2016–2017

September and October 2016 marked the beginning of the most frightening period of my recovery journey. What started as tightness around my neck, shoulders and upper back eventually became a period when even walking, standing and sleeping could feel difficult.

HomeMy Recovery StoryComputer Work & My Neck
September–October 2016

When Everything Changed

Before this period, I was mainly focused on my career. I had started my IT career in 2014 and was spending long hours working at a computer. I did not pay enough attention to exercise, posture, breaks or the condition of my body.

At first, I noticed tightness around my neck, shoulders and upper back. I did not understand what was happening and initially thought it might simply be weakness or tiredness.

Then vertigo appeared. That was the point when my experience became much more frightening.

“I was no longer thinking about career growth. My focus became much simpler — being able to stand, walk, sleep and get through the day.”
The Difficult Phase

Vertigo, Vomiting and Difficulty Moving Around

During the worst period, I experienced vertigo along with vomiting. There were times when I was not able to walk or stand up normally. Sleeping also became difficult.

It was a horrible and frightening experience. I became worried about going far from my place because I did not know when another episode of vertigo might happen.

Things that previously felt normal — travelling, going somewhere far away or simply moving around confidently — suddenly became difficult.

2016–2017

My Recovery Was Gradual

I started physiotherapy in 2016. During this period I also used medicines as advised to me. At different points I took Vertin (8 mg or 16 mg) and other medicines. I also visited doctors, including a doctor in Karnal, Haryana.

Recovery was not a straight line. There were periods when I continued physiotherapy and periods when I stopped and later started again. I gradually began paying more attention to rest, walking, stretching and how I was using my body.

I learned that recovery for me was not about one single thing. It was about changing several parts of my daily life and giving myself time.

What I Changed

Exercise, Walking, Rest and Less Screen Time

Stretching and exerciseI gradually made exercise and stretching a regular part of my routine.
WalkingWalking became one of the simple activities I used as I gradually became more comfortable moving around again.
RestI learned to rest when I felt tired instead of constantly pushing myself to continue.
Reducing screen exposureDuring the difficult phase, I significantly reduced laptop, phone and television use and focused more on recovery.
PostureI started paying attention to how I sat, slept and worked.
A Different Priority

I Started Focusing on Myself

At one stage, I left my job and focused more on myself, exercise, walking, rest and rebuilding my confidence.

I also started looking for ways to stay active without putting all my attention on work. Cricket became one of those activities. I had experience with cricket, and I started doing local cricket umpiring.

These activities helped me feel that my life was becoming bigger than the symptoms I had been experiencing.

Recovery gave me something I had lost during the worst period: confidence that I could gradually return to normal life.
Looking Back

What That Period Taught Me

Looking back, I understand how easy it is to focus completely on a career and ignore small changes in the body. In my case, I had spent years working at a computer with poor posture and very little regular exercise.

I cannot say that one specific habit caused everything I experienced. My story is personal, and there were several things happening in my life at the time. But the experience changed the way I think about work, posture, exercise, rest and stress.

Today I am able to travel to the office, drive a car, use public transport and continue working as a developer. I still pay attention to my posture and daily habits because I do not want to return to the way I used to work.

A note about this story

This article describes my personal experience and the changes that I found helpful during my recovery. It is not medical advice, a diagnosis, or a treatment plan. Symptoms such as severe dizziness, vomiting, difficulty walking or other concerning symptoms can have many causes. Anyone experiencing significant or persistent symptoms should seek appropriate medical evaluation.
