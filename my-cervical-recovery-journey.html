My Cervical Pain Recovery Story: From Severe Symptoms in 2016 to Returning to IT

My personal cervical pain recovery story, including severe symptoms and vertigo in 2016, physiotherapy, lifestyle changes, posture, exercise and my journey back to working as an IT developer.

By Shobhit Kaushik · Personal Experience · 2016–2026

Important: This is my personal experience, not medical advice. My symptoms and treatment were specific to me. If you experience persistent, severe or unusual symptoms, please seek appropriate medical evaluation.

How My Computer-Work Lifestyle Started

I started my IT career in 2014 in Delhi.

I was excited about my career and wanted to learn, grow and build something for myself.

As a developer, spending long hours in front of a computer became a normal part of my life.

I would sit in front of my laptop and become completely focused on my work. Sometimes I could work for 5–6 hours continuously without properly thinking about my posture or taking a break.

I wasn't thinking about my neck. I wasn't thinking about exercise. I wasn't thinking about how long I had been sitting.

I was thinking about my work.

At that time, I didn't understand how important these things could become.

The First Signs of My Neck and Upper-Back Problems

Around September–October 2016, something started changing.

Initially, it wasn't the severe pain that I would later experience. I started feeling a kind of tightness around my neck, shoulders and upper back.

Sometimes I would feel better when I was away from work. For example, on a Sunday, when I wasn't working, I would feel more relaxed.

At the time, I didn't connect these things with my working habits.

I continued with my normal routine.

And then something much worse happened.

When Vertigo and Severe Symptoms Started

After coming back from work, I started experiencing vertigo.

Initially, I thought maybe I was weak or tired. I didn't realize that this was going to become the beginning of one of the most difficult periods of my life.

The symptoms became much more severe.

  • Severe pain
  • Vertigo
  • Vomiting
  • Difficulty standing
  • Difficulty walking
  • Difficulty sleeping
  • A feeling of extreme discomfort and fear

It was a horrible experience.

There were times when even basic things that I had previously taken for granted became difficult.

2016–2017: The Worst Period of My Recovery Journey

The period from 2016 to 2017 was the hardest part of my journey.

I was no longer thinking about career growth. I was thinking about getting through the day.

I became afraid of travelling far from home because I didn't know when the vertigo might happen.

My normal life became very restricted.

During this period, I started physiotherapy and also consulted doctors. I also visited doctors outside Delhi, including doctors in Karnal, Haryana, trying to understand and manage what was happening.

I took medicines that were prescribed to me during different periods of my treatment, including Vertin at 8 mg and 16 mg at different times, along with other medicines.

I mention these medicines only because they were part of my own treatment history. I am not recommending them to anyone.

Physiotherapy and My Recovery Timeline

I started physiotherapy during the early period of my problems.

My recovery wasn't immediate.

There were periods when I stopped physiotherapy and later started again. Around 2020, I went through physiotherapy again.

Looking back, one thing I learned is that recovery isn't always a straight line.

Improvement → setback → treatment → improvement → lifestyle change → recovery

It took me years to understand this.

What Helped Me During My Recovery

Eventually, I realized that I couldn't simply continue living exactly the same way.

I started paying much more attention to my lifestyle.

I began focusing on:

  • Stretching
  • Walking
  • Exercise
  • Rest
  • Posture
  • Reducing screen time

I started listening to my body more carefully.

If I was tired, I tried to rest instead of simply pushing through.

Taking a Career Break to Focus on Myself

One of the biggest decisions I made was to leave my job for a period of time.

This was not easy. My career was important to me.

But at that stage, I felt that I needed to focus on myself.

For some time, I significantly reduced my use of technology. I avoided unnecessary laptop, phone and television use.

I spent more time walking and exercising. I walked twice a day.

My focus shifted from “How can I work more?” to “How can I get myself back?”

Getting Active Again Through Cricket

Something else important happened during this period.

I started doing cricket umpiring in local matches.

I have some experience with cricket, so this gave me something to do that was outside the computer environment.

It got me outside. It gave me an activity to focus on. It helped me feel connected with normal life again.

For me, recovery wasn't only about treatment. It was also about slowly getting back into life.

Returning to Work and Rebuilding My IT Career

As things improved, I started thinking about my career again.

I began looking for another job.

But this time I had learned something important.

I didn't want to completely sacrifice my health for my career.

A career + a healthy lifestyle

How I Returned to IT Work

Eventually, I returned to professional IT work.

Today, I am working as a developer in an MNC.

I go to the office. I drive my car. I travel. I work with computers. I continue my career.

And I exercise regularly.

When I compare this with 2016–2017, the difference is enormous.

There was a time when I was afraid to travel far from home because I didn't know when vertigo might occur.

Today, I can drive to the office and continue my professional life.

What I Do Today to Protect My Neck While Working

I still work on computers because I am a developer. I didn't quit technology.

Instead, I changed how I work.

  • Proper posture
  • A larger screen
  • Ergonomic keyboard
  • Ergonomic mouse
  • Comfortable screen distance
  • Regular movement
  • Exercise
  • Stretching
  • Rest
  • Taking breaks
  • Better stress management

I currently exercise twice a day and also try to move around during my lunch break.

I don't want to go back to the habit of sitting continuously for hours with my neck pushed forward toward a laptop.

My Biggest Lesson From Cervical Pain

My experience taught me something I wish I had understood when I started my career in 2014.

“Work hard for your career, but don't forget the body that is helping you build it.”

I was so focused on my career that I didn't pay enough attention to my posture, exercise, breaks or overall lifestyle.

I can't change what happened in 2016.

But I can change what I do today.

Today, I Don't Take Normal Life for Granted

Today, sitting at a computer and writing code may look like an ordinary part of my life.

Driving to the office may look ordinary. Going to work may look ordinary. Travelling may look ordinary.

But after what I experienced, I see these things differently.

I know what it feels like when ordinary life suddenly becomes difficult.

That's why today I value my health, my career and my everyday life much more.

I didn't give up my career.

I learned how to live differently while continuing it.
