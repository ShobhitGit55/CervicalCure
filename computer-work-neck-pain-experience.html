How Long Hours at a Computer Affected My Neck: My Experience as a Developer

A personal story about my computer-work habits, poor posture, lack of exercise and the changes I made after experiencing neck problems.

By Shobhit Kaushik · Personal Experience

Important: This article describes my personal experience. It is not medical advice, and I am not saying that computer work caused my symptoms. If you have persistent or severe symptoms, please seek appropriate medical evaluation.

My Life as an IT Developer Started in 2014

I started my IT career in 2014 in Delhi.

Like many developers, I became comfortable spending a large part of my day in front of a computer. My priority was to learn, improve my skills, complete my work and build my career.

At that time, I didn't think much about how I was sitting or how often I was moving.

How I Used to Work for 5–6 Hours Without a Proper Break

When I became deeply focused on a task, I could sit in front of my laptop for 5–6 hours continuously.

I wasn't taking proper breaks. I wasn't drinking enough water. I wasn't thinking about stretching.

My attention was completely on the job.

At that stage, I believed that staying focused and working continuously was a sign of being serious about my career.

My Poor Computer Posture

One of the biggest things I now recognize about my old routine was my posture.

I would lean forward toward the laptop and look carefully at the screen without paying attention to keeping my neck in a comfortable position.

My neck would gradually move forward while I remained focused on the screen.

I didn't have the workstation setup I use today.

I Wasn't Exercising Regularly

Another mistake was that I had almost no regular exercise routine when I started my career.

After spending hours sitting and working, I wasn't doing enough to compensate for all that inactivity.

There was no mandatory morning exercise routine.

Walking and stretching were not a regular part of my day.

What I did then

  • Long computer sessions
  • Poor posture
  • Little exercise
  • Few breaks
  • Long phone use

What I do now

  • Better posture
  • Regular exercise
  • Movement breaks
  • Larger screen
  • Ergonomic equipment

Phone, Laptop and Screen Time Were Also Part of My Routine

My laptop wasn't the only screen I used for long periods.

I also spent a lot of time using my phone and watching television.

Looking back, I realize that my overall screen-heavy lifestyle meant that my neck and body were not getting enough movement throughout the day.

Today, I try to be much more conscious about unnecessary screen time outside work.

Driving and Sitting Added More Time to My Day

I also drove a car and bike.

Driving meant more time sitting, especially when combined with a full day of computer work.

At the time, I didn't think of all these small periods of sitting as part of one larger daily routine.

The First Warning Signs

Before the worst period began, I started noticing that my body felt different.

I remember feeling tightness around my neck, shoulders and upper back.

Sometimes I felt more relaxed on a Sunday when I wasn't working.

I also experienced stress during that period, but I prefer to keep the personal details private.

At first, I didn't understand what these signs meant.

Then My Symptoms Became Much More Serious

Around September–October 2016, my experience changed dramatically.

I started experiencing vertigo, and the symptoms became severe enough that walking and standing could become difficult. I also experienced vomiting and difficulty sleeping.

That period eventually became the worst part of my journey from 2016 to 2017.

I have described that part of my story in detail in my main recovery article.

I was focused on building my career, but I had forgotten to take care of the person doing the work.

What Changed My Thinking About Computer Work

My experience changed the way I look at working in IT.

I did not decide that computers were the problem or that I had to leave software development forever.

Instead, I realized that the way I work matters.

My Computer Setup Today

Today, I still work as a developer and spend significant time at a computer.

But my working environment is very different.

  • I use a larger screen instead of constantly looking down at a laptop.
  • I maintain a more comfortable and upright neck and sitting posture.
  • I use an ergonomic keyboard and mouse.
  • I keep the screen at a comfortable distance and height.
  • I take movement and rest breaks instead of sitting continuously for hours.
  • I exercise regularly.
  • I exercise twice a day as part of my current routine.
  • I also try to move during my lunch break.

I Still Work in IT — I Just Work Differently

This is probably the most important part of my story.

I didn't have to give up my career.

Today, I work as a developer in an MNC. I can go to the office, drive, work with computers and continue building my career.

But I no longer want work to completely control my lifestyle.

What I Would Tell Myself in 2014

Don't wait for your body to force you to change.

Take care of your posture, move regularly, exercise and take breaks while building your career.

My Biggest Lesson From Working at a Computer

For years, I thought that the most important thing was to work harder and stay focused.

Now I think differently.

Being productive is important, but so is being able to continue doing the work for many years.

For me, the goal is no longer simply “Work as much as possible.”

It is “Build a career while taking care of myself.”
